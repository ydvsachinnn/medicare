# Common Medicines Information Guide (Educational Reference)

## IMPORTANT DISCLAIMER
This information is for educational purposes ONLY. Never self-medicate. Always consult a qualified healthcare professional before starting, stopping, or changing any medication. Dosages vary based on age, weight, medical conditions, and other factors.

---

## Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)

### Uses
- Fever reduction (antipyretic)
- Mild to moderate pain relief (headache, toothache, muscle pain, menstrual cramps)
- Common cold and flu symptom relief

### General Dosage Information
- Adults: 500mg-1000mg every 4-6 hours as needed (maximum 4g/day)
- Children: Dosage based on weight and age — always consult pediatrician
- Available as tablets, liquid suspension, suppositories

### Side Effects
- Generally well-tolerated at recommended doses
- Rare: skin rash, allergic reactions
- Overdose risk: severe liver damage (especially with alcohol use)

### Warnings
- Do NOT exceed recommended daily dose
- Avoid with chronic alcohol use
- Caution in liver disease patients
- Check other medications for paracetamol content to avoid double-dosing
- Seek emergency care if overdose suspected

## Ibuprofen

### Uses
- Pain relief (headache, dental pain, menstrual cramps, muscle pain, arthritis)
- Fever reduction
- Anti-inflammatory (reduces swelling)

### General Information
- NSAID (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug)
- Take with food or milk to reduce stomach irritation
- Available as tablets, capsules, liquid suspension, topical gel

### Side Effects
- Stomach pain, nausea, heartburn
- Increased risk of stomach ulcers and GI bleeding
- Dizziness, headache
- Kidney problems with prolonged use
- Increased blood pressure

### Warnings
- Avoid in patients with stomach ulcers or GI bleeding
- Use with caution in kidney disease, heart disease, hypertension
- NOT recommended during dengue (increases bleeding risk)
- Avoid during late pregnancy (third trimester)
- Do not combine with other NSAIDs or blood thinners without medical advice

## Amoxicillin

### Uses
- Bacterial infections: ear infections, sinus infections, urinary tract infections, throat infections, pneumonia, bronchitis, skin infections, dental infections

### General Information
- Penicillin-type antibiotic
- Available as capsules, tablets, liquid suspension
- Usually taken 2-3 times daily for 5-14 days
- Complete the full course even if symptoms improve

### Side Effects
- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
- Skin rash (may indicate allergy)
- Yeast infections (candidiasis)
- Stomach discomfort

### Warnings
- DO NOT use if allergic to penicillin or cephalosporins
- Inform doctor about all other medications
- May reduce effectiveness of oral contraceptives
- Seek immediate care if severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis): difficulty breathing, swelling of face/throat, hives

## Metformin

### Uses
- Type 2 diabetes management
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
- Prediabetes prevention

### General Information
- First-line medication for Type 2 diabetes
- Works by reducing liver glucose production and improving insulin sensitivity
- Take with meals to reduce GI side effects
- Available as immediate-release and extended-release tablets

### Side Effects
- Nausea, diarrhea, stomach cramps (common initially, usually improves)
- Metallic taste in mouth
- Vitamin B12 deficiency with long-term use
- Rare but serious: lactic acidosis (seek emergency care if muscle pain, difficulty breathing, unusual fatigue)

### Warnings
- NOT for Type 1 diabetes
- Avoid in severe kidney disease (eGFR below 30)
- Temporarily discontinue before contrast dye procedures
- Avoid excessive alcohol
- Monitor kidney function regularly

## Amlodipine

### Uses
- High blood pressure (hypertension)
- Chest pain (angina)
- Coronary artery disease

### General Information
- Calcium channel blocker
- Usually taken once daily
- May take 1-2 weeks for full effect on blood pressure
- Do not stop abruptly

### Side Effects
- Swelling of ankles/feet (edema)
- Dizziness and lightheadedness
- Flushing (feeling of warmth)
- Fatigue
- Palpitations

### Warnings
- Monitor blood pressure regularly
- Avoid grapefruit and grapefruit juice (increases drug levels)
- Caution in liver disease
- Inform doctor before surgery

## Omeprazole

### Uses
- Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)
- Stomach and duodenal ulcers
- H. pylori eradication (with antibiotics)
- Acid-related stomach disorders
- Prevention of NSAID-induced ulcers

### General Information
- Proton pump inhibitor (PPI)
- Take 30 minutes before breakfast on empty stomach
- Capsules should be swallowed whole, not crushed

### Side Effects
- Headache, nausea, diarrhea, constipation
- Abdominal pain, gas
- Long-term use: Vitamin B12 and magnesium deficiency, increased fracture risk

### Warnings
- Not recommended for long-term use without medical supervision
- May interact with blood thinners (clopidogrel)
- Report severe diarrhea (possible C. difficile infection)
- Monitor magnesium levels with prolonged use

## Cetirizine

### Uses
- Allergic rhinitis (hay fever)
- Hives (urticaria)
- Itching and allergic skin conditions
- Seasonal and perennial allergies

### General Information
- Second-generation antihistamine
- Available as tablets, liquid, and chewable forms
- Usually taken once daily

### Side Effects
- Drowsiness (less than first-generation antihistamines)
- Dry mouth
- Headache
- Fatigue

### Warnings
- May cause drowsiness; use caution when driving
- Avoid alcohol
- Adjust dose in kidney disease

---

## Azithromycin

### Uses
- Respiratory tract infections (bronchitis, pneumonia)
- Ear infections
- Skin infections
- Sexually transmitted infections
- Typhoid fever

### General Information
- Macrolide antibiotic
- Usually taken once daily for 3-5 days
- Can be taken with or without food
- Complete the full course

### Side Effects
- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
- Abdominal pain
- Headache
- Rare: hearing changes, liver problems

### Warnings
- Inform doctor about heart conditions (risk of QT prolongation)
- May interact with blood thinners and other medications
- Seek care if severe allergic reaction, irregular heartbeat, or jaundice

---

## Aspirin (Low-Dose)

### Uses
- Prevention of heart attack and stroke (in high-risk patients as prescribed)
- Pain relief and fever reduction (higher doses)
- Anti-inflammatory

### General Information
- Low-dose (75-100mg) for cardiovascular prevention
- Take with food to reduce stomach irritation

### Side Effects
- Stomach irritation and GI bleeding
- Easy bruising
- Tinnitus (ringing in ears) at high doses

### Warnings
- NOT recommended for children (Reye's syndrome risk)
- Avoid in dengue fever (increases bleeding risk)
- Do not take with other blood thinners without medical advice
- Inform surgeon/dentist before procedures
- Do not start low-dose aspirin without doctor's recommendation

## Salbutamol (Albuterol)

### Uses
- Asthma relief (rescue inhaler)
- Bronchospasm relief
- Exercise-induced bronchoconstriction
- Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)

### General Information
- Short-acting beta-agonist (SABA)
- Available as metered-dose inhaler, nebulizer solution, tablets
- Provides quick relief within minutes
- Carry rescue inhaler at all times if prescribed

### Side Effects
- Tremor (shaking hands)
- Rapid heartbeat
- Headache
- Nervousness
- Throat irritation (inhaler)

### Warnings
- If needing rescue inhaler more than 2 times per week, asthma may be poorly controlled
- Do not exceed recommended doses
- Rinse mouth after using inhaler to prevent oral thrush
- Seek emergency care if no improvement after using inhaler

## CRITICAL REMINDER
NEVER self-medicate. All medication decisions must be made in consultation with a licensed healthcare professional. Dosages, contraindications, and interactions vary based on individual health conditions.
