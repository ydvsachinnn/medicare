# Comprehensive Disease Guide — Part 1: Metabolic & Chronic Diseases (WHO/NIH/CDC)

## Diabetes Mellitus

### Overview
Diabetes mellitus is a group of chronic metabolic diseases characterized by elevated blood glucose (blood sugar) levels resulting from defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both. Over time, uncontrolled diabetes leads to serious damage to the heart, blood vessels, eyes, kidneys, and nerves.

### Types
- **Type 1 Diabetes:** An autoimmune condition where the pancreas produces little or no insulin. Usually diagnosed in children and young adults. Requires daily insulin injections.
- **Type 2 Diabetes:** The body does not use insulin properly (insulin resistance). Most common form (90-95% of cases). Often associated with obesity and sedentary lifestyle.
- **Gestational Diabetes:** Develops during pregnancy. Usually resolves after delivery but increases future risk of Type 2 diabetes.
- **Prediabetes:** Blood sugar levels higher than normal but not yet high enough for a Type 2 diagnosis. HbA1c between 5.7% and 6.4%.

### Causes & Risk Factors
- Family history of diabetes
- Obesity or overweight (BMI above 25)
- Sedentary lifestyle and physical inactivity
- Age above 45 years
- History of gestational diabetes
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
- High blood pressure (above 140/90 mmHg)
- Abnormal cholesterol levels

### Symptoms
- Excessive thirst (polydipsia)
- Frequent urination (polyuria)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Extreme fatigue and weakness
- Blurry or distorted vision
- Slow-healing wounds and frequent infections
- Tingling or numbness in hands/feet (peripheral neuropathy)
- Darkened skin patches (acanthosis nigricans)

### Diagnosis
- **Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS):** Normal: below 100 mg/dL; Prediabetes: 100-125 mg/dL; Diabetes: 126 mg/dL or higher.
- **HbA1c Test:** Normal: below 5.7%; Prediabetes: 5.7%-6.4%; Diabetes: 6.5% or higher.
- **Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT):** Measures blood sugar before and 2 hours after drinking a glucose solution.
- **Random Blood Sugar Test:** Diabetes: 200 mg/dL or higher with symptoms.

### Treatment Options
- Lifestyle modifications (diet and exercise)
- Oral medications (Metformin, Sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors)
- Insulin therapy (for Type 1 and advanced Type 2)
- Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM)
- Regular medical checkups every 3-6 months

### Diet Recommendations
- Choose low glycemic index (GI) foods: whole grains, legumes, non-starchy vegetables
- Include high-fiber foods: oats, brown rice, quinoa, leafy greens
- Lean proteins: fish, chicken, tofu, eggs
- Healthy fats: olive oil, nuts, avocado
- Avoid: refined sugars, white bread, sodas, fruit juices, processed snacks
- Limit alcohol consumption
- Eat at regular intervals; do not skip meals

### Complications
- Heart disease and stroke
- Kidney disease (diabetic nephropathy)
- Eye damage (diabetic retinopathy) leading to blindness
- Nerve damage (diabetic neuropathy)
- Foot ulcers and potential amputation
- Skin infections

### Emergency Warning Signs
- Very high blood sugar (above 300 mg/dL) with confusion
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA): fruity breath, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain
- Severe hypoglycemia: shakiness, sweating, confusion, seizures, loss of consciousness

## Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

### Overview
Hypertension is defined as persistently elevated arterial blood pressure. It is a major risk factor for heart disease, stroke, kidney failure, and other serious health conditions. Often called the "silent killer" because it typically has no symptoms until significant damage has occurred.

### Blood Pressure Categories
- **Normal:** Less than 120/80 mmHg
- **Elevated:** 120-129/less than 80 mmHg
- **Stage 1 Hypertension:** 130-139/80-89 mmHg
- **Stage 2 Hypertension:** 140/90 mmHg or higher
- **Hypertensive Crisis:** Higher than 180/120 mmHg (seek emergency care immediately)

### Causes & Risk Factors
- Family history of hypertension
- Age (risk increases with age)
- Obesity and overweight
- High sodium (salt) diet
- Physical inactivity
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Chronic stress
- Smoking
- Sleep apnea
- Chronic kidney disease

### Symptoms
- Often asymptomatic (silent)
- Severe cases: morning headaches, dizziness, nosebleeds
- Vision changes and blurred vision
- Chest pain and shortness of breath (emergency)
- Blood in urine (advanced kidney damage)

### Diagnosis
- Blood pressure measurement using a sphygmomanometer
- 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring
- Blood tests: kidney function, cholesterol, blood sugar
- ECG (Electrocardiogram) to assess heart function
- Echocardiogram if heart damage is suspected

### Treatment Options
- Lifestyle modifications (DASH diet, exercise, weight loss)
- ACE inhibitors (e.g., Enalapril, Lisinopril)
- ARBs (e.g., Losartan, Valsartan)
- Calcium channel blockers (e.g., Amlodipine)
- Diuretics (e.g., Hydrochlorothiazide)
- Beta-blockers (e.g., Metoprolol)
- Regular blood pressure monitoring at home

### Diet Recommendations (DASH Diet)
- Reduce sodium intake to less than 2,300 mg/day (ideally 1,500 mg/day)
- Increase potassium-rich foods: bananas, sweet potatoes, spinach, beans
- Whole grains, fruits, and vegetables
- Low-fat dairy products
- Lean poultry, fish, and nuts
- Limit red meats, sweets, and saturated fats
- Avoid processed and packaged foods

### Emergency Warning Signs
- Blood pressure above 180/120 mmHg
- Severe headache with confusion
- Chest pain or difficulty breathing
- Vision changes or loss
- Weakness or numbness (stroke signs)

## Thyroid Disorders

### Overview
The thyroid gland produces hormones (T3 and T4) that regulate metabolism, energy production, heart rate, and body temperature. Thyroid disorders include hypothyroidism (underactive) and hyperthyroidism (overactive).

### Hypothyroidism
- **Causes:** Hashimoto's thyroiditis (autoimmune), iodine deficiency, thyroid surgery, radiation therapy
- **Symptoms:** Fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, dry skin, hair loss, constipation, depression, muscle weakness, irregular periods
- **Treatment:** Levothyroxine (synthetic thyroid hormone replacement)

### Hyperthyroidism
- **Causes:** Graves' disease (autoimmune), thyroid nodules, thyroiditis
- **Symptoms:** Weight loss, rapid heartbeat, anxiety, tremors, heat intolerance, sweating, diarrhea, bulging eyes (Graves' disease)
- **Treatment:** Anti-thyroid medications (Methimazole), radioactive iodine therapy, beta-blockers for symptom control, surgery in severe cases

### Diagnosis
- **TSH Test:** Primary screening test. High TSH indicates hypothyroidism; low TSH indicates hyperthyroidism.
- **Free T4 and Free T3 Tests:** Measure active thyroid hormone levels.
- **Thyroid Antibody Tests:** Detect autoimmune thyroid disease.
- **Thyroid Ultrasound:** Evaluates thyroid gland size, nodules, and structural abnormalities.

### When to Consult a Doctor
- Unexplained weight changes
- Persistent fatigue not relieved by rest
- Neck swelling or visible lump
- Rapid or irregular heartbeat
- Significant mood or cognitive changes

## Obesity

### Overview
Obesity is a complex chronic disease defined as having a BMI of 30 or higher. It increases risk of heart disease, diabetes, stroke, certain cancers, and many other conditions.

### BMI Categories
- **Underweight:** Below 18.5
- **Normal Weight:** 18.5 to 24.9
- **Overweight:** 25.0 to 29.9
- **Obesity Class I:** 30.0 to 34.9
- **Obesity Class II:** 35.0 to 39.9
- **Obesity Class III (Morbid):** 40.0 or higher

### Causes & Risk Factors
- Excessive caloric intake
- Sedentary lifestyle
- Genetic predisposition
- Hormonal factors (hypothyroidism, PCOS, Cushing's syndrome)
- Medications (certain antidepressants, steroids)
- Sleep deprivation
- Stress and emotional eating

### Health Complications
- Type 2 diabetes
- Heart disease and high blood pressure
- Stroke
- Sleep apnea
- Osteoarthritis
- Fatty liver disease
- Certain cancers (breast, colon, kidney)
- Depression and reduced quality of life

### Treatment Approach
- Dietary changes: caloric deficit, balanced nutrition
- Regular physical activity: 150-300 minutes of moderate exercise per week
- Behavioral therapy and counseling
- Medical weight management under physician supervision
- Bariatric surgery for severe cases (BMI above 40 or BMI above 35 with complications)

### Diet for Weight Loss
- High protein, moderate carbs, healthy fats
- Vegetables, fruits, lean meats, whole grains
- Avoid sugary drinks, fast food, fried foods
- Portion control and mindful eating
- Drink adequate water (8-10 glasses per day)

## PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome)

### Overview
PCOS is a hormonal disorder common among women of reproductive age. It involves infrequent, irregular, or prolonged menstrual periods and excess male hormone (androgen) levels. The ovaries may develop numerous small collections of fluid (follicles) and fail to regularly release eggs.

### Symptoms
- Irregular or missed periods
- Heavy menstrual bleeding
- Excess facial and body hair (hirsutism)
- Acne (face, chest, back)
- Weight gain, especially around the abdomen
- Thinning hair on the scalp
- Darkened skin patches (neck, groin, under breasts)
- Difficulty getting pregnant (infertility)

### Causes & Risk Factors
- Insulin resistance
- Hormonal imbalance (excess androgens)
- Genetic factors and family history
- Obesity
- Chronic low-grade inflammation

### Diagnosis
- Medical history and symptom evaluation
- Pelvic examination
- Blood tests: hormone levels (testosterone, LH, FSH), blood sugar, insulin
- Pelvic ultrasound: to check ovaries for cysts

### Treatment
- Lifestyle changes: weight loss, regular exercise, healthy diet
- Birth control pills to regulate periods
- Metformin for insulin resistance
- Anti-androgen medications
- Fertility treatments if trying to conceive

### Diet Recommendations
- Low glycemic index foods
- Anti-inflammatory diet: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, fatty fish
- Avoid processed foods, refined carbs, and excess sugar
- Regular small meals throughout the day
