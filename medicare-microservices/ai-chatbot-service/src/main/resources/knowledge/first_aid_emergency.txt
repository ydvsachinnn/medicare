# Comprehensive First Aid & Emergency Guide (CDC/NIH/Red Cross)

## Burns

### First-Degree Burns (Superficial)
- Affects only outer layer of skin (epidermis)
- Symptoms: Redness, pain, mild swelling
- Treatment: Cool under running water for 10-15 minutes. Apply aloe vera or moisturizer. Cover with sterile non-stick bandage. Take paracetamol for pain.
- DO NOT apply ice, butter, or toothpaste

### Second-Degree Burns
- Affects epidermis and part of dermis
- Symptoms: Red, blistered, swollen, very painful
- Treatment: Cool under running water for 15-20 minutes. DO NOT pop blisters. Cover with sterile non-stick bandage. Seek medical attention.
- Watch for signs of infection: increased pain, redness, swelling, fever

### Third-Degree Burns (Severe — EMERGENCY)
- Affects all layers of skin and underlying tissue
- Symptoms: White, charred, or leathery skin. May be painless due to nerve damage.
- Treatment: Call emergency services immediately. Do NOT remove clothing stuck to burn. Cover with cool, moist sterile cloth. Elevate burned area above heart if possible. Monitor for shock.

## Snake Bites

### Immediate Actions
1. Move the person away from the snake
2. Keep the person calm and still (movement spreads venom faster)
3. Immobilize the bitten limb below heart level
4. Remove jewelry, watches, tight clothing near bite site (swelling will occur)
5. Clean the bite wound gently with soap and water
6. Mark the edge of swelling with a pen and note the time
7. Transport to hospital IMMEDIATELY for antivenom

### DO NOT
- Do NOT cut the wound
- Do NOT try to suck out venom
- Do NOT apply a tight tourniquet (pressure immobilization bandage is acceptable for some species)
- Do NOT apply ice or cold pack
- Do NOT give aspirin or ibuprofen (increases bleeding)
- Do NOT give alcohol

### Important
- Try to note the snake's appearance (color, size, pattern) for identification
- Antivenom is the only effective treatment — seek emergency room immediately

## Dog Bites & Rabies Prevention

### Immediate Actions
1. Wash the wound IMMEDIATELY with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes (reduces rabies viral load significantly)
2. Apply antiseptic (povidone-iodine or alcohol)
3. Apply sterile bandage
4. Note the dog's vaccination status and behavior (provoked vs unprovoked)

### Medical Care (within 24 hours)
- Tetanus booster if not up to date
- Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) rabies vaccine series if:
  - Dog is stray or vaccination status unknown
  - Dog is behaving abnormally
  - Unprovoked attack
- Antibiotics may be prescribed for deep wounds
- Rabies immunoglobulin (RIG) may be administered around the wound

### Warning Signs of Infection
- Increasing pain, redness, warmth around the wound
- Swelling and pus discharge
- Fever
- Red streaks extending from the wound

## Bleeding Control

### Minor Bleeding
1. Wash hands and wear gloves if available
2. Apply direct pressure with clean cloth or sterile gauze
3. Keep pressure for 10-15 minutes without lifting
4. Elevate the injured area above heart level if possible
5. Apply bandage once bleeding stops
6. Clean wound with mild soap and water

### Severe Bleeding (EMERGENCY)
1. Call emergency services immediately
2. Apply firm, direct pressure with clean cloth or gauze
3. If blood soaks through, add more layers on top (do NOT remove first layer)
4. Apply tourniquet 2-3 inches above wound if bleeding is life-threatening and cannot be controlled with direct pressure
5. Keep the person lying down and calm
6. Monitor for signs of shock: pale skin, rapid breathing, confusion, cold clammy skin

## Fractures (Broken Bones)

### Signs of a Fracture
- Severe pain at injury site
- Swelling and bruising
- Deformity or unnatural angle
- Inability to move the affected limb
- Grinding sensation
- Bone protruding through skin (open fracture — EMERGENCY)

### First Aid
1. Do NOT attempt to realign the bone
2. Immobilize the injured area in the position found
3. Apply ice wrapped in cloth (20 minutes on, 20 minutes off) to reduce swelling
4. Splint the fracture if trained (use padded rigid material)
5. Elevate the injured area if possible
6. Control any bleeding with sterile dressing
7. Monitor circulation below the injury (pulse, sensation, movement)
8. Transport to hospital

### Open Fracture (Bone Exposed)
- Cover wound with sterile moist dressing
- Do NOT push bone back in
- Call emergency services immediately
- Control bleeding with gentle pressure around (not on) the wound

## Heat Stroke (EMERGENCY)

### Signs
- Body temperature above 104°F (40°C)
- Hot, red, dry skin (sweating may have stopped)
- Rapid, strong pulse
- Confusion, altered mental state
- Headache, dizziness, nausea
- Loss of consciousness

### Treatment
1. Call emergency services immediately
2. Move person to cool, shaded area
3. Cool rapidly: apply cool water to skin, place ice packs on neck, armpits, and groin
4. Fan the person while misting with cool water
5. Do NOT give fluids if unconscious
6. Monitor body temperature continuously
7. Place in recovery position if unconscious

### Prevention
- Stay hydrated in hot weather
- Avoid prolonged sun exposure during peak hours (10 AM - 4 PM)
- Wear lightweight, light-colored clothing
- Take frequent breaks in shade
- Never leave anyone in a parked car

## Electric Shock

### Immediate Actions
1. Do NOT touch the person if they are still in contact with electrical source
2. Turn off power source or use a dry, non-conductive object (wooden stick, rubber) to separate person from source
3. Call emergency services immediately
4. Check for breathing and pulse — start CPR if needed
5. Look for entry and exit burn wounds
6. Cover burns with sterile dressing
7. Monitor for cardiac arrhythmias (heart may need monitoring for 24 hours)

### Warning
- Even minor electric shocks can cause internal injuries
- Seek medical evaluation even if the person appears fine
- Lightning strikes: treat as severe electric shock emergency

## Poisoning

### Ingested Poison
1. Call poison control center or emergency services immediately
2. Try to identify the substance, amount, and time of ingestion
3. Do NOT induce vomiting unless specifically instructed by poison control
4. Do NOT give anything to eat or drink unless instructed
5. If the person is conscious and not convulsing, you may give sips of water
6. Save the container or remaining substance for identification

### Inhaled Poison (Carbon Monoxide, Gas Leaks)
1. Move person to fresh air immediately
2. Call emergency services
3. Begin CPR if not breathing
4. Keep the person warm and calm

## Food Poisoning

### Symptoms (appear within hours to days)
- Nausea and vomiting
- Diarrhea (possibly bloody)
- Abdominal cramps
- Fever
- Dehydration

### Treatment
- Stay hydrated: ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution), clear fluids, coconut water
- Rest the stomach: start with bland foods (BRAT diet — bananas, rice, applesauce, toast)
- Avoid dairy, caffeine, alcohol, spicy foods
- Seek medical care if: high fever, blood in stool, severe dehydration, symptoms lasting more than 3 days, pregnant, elderly, or immunocompromised

## Choking

### For Adults and Children Over 1 Year (Heimlich Maneuver)
1. Stand behind the person and wrap arms around their waist
2. Make a fist with one hand and place it above the navel, below the breastbone
3. Grasp your fist with your other hand
4. Perform quick, upward abdominal thrusts
5. Repeat until object is expelled or person becomes unconscious
6. If unconscious: lower person to ground, call emergency services, begin CPR

### For Infants Under 1 Year
1. Place infant face down on your forearm, supporting head
2. Give 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades with heel of hand
3. Turn infant face up, give 5 chest thrusts using 2 fingers on center of chest
4. Alternate back blows and chest thrusts until object is expelled
5. If infant becomes unconscious: call emergency, begin infant CPR

## CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)

### For Adults
1. Check responsiveness: tap shoulders and shout "Are you okay?"
2. Call emergency services (or have someone call)
3. Check for breathing (look, listen, feel for 10 seconds)
4. If not breathing: begin chest compressions
   - Place heel of one hand on center of chest (between nipples)
   - Place other hand on top, interlace fingers
   - Push hard and fast: at least 2 inches deep, 100-120 compressions per minute
5. If trained: give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions
6. If untrained: perform hands-only CPR (continuous chest compressions)
7. Continue until emergency services arrive or the person starts breathing
8. Use an AED (Automated External Defibrillator) if available — follow voice prompts

This information is for educational purposes only. In any emergency, always call local emergency services immediately. This guide does not replace professional first aid training.
