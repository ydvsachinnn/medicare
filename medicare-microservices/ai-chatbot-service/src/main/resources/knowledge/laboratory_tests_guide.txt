# Laboratory Tests & Medical Investigations Guide (NIH/WHO)

## Complete Blood Count (CBC)

### What It Is
A CBC is one of the most commonly ordered blood tests. It measures the number and types of cells in your blood, including red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets.

### Components
- **Red Blood Cell (RBC) Count:** Normal: Men 4.5-5.5 million/mcL; Women 4.0-5.0 million/mcL
- **Hemoglobin (Hb):** Normal: Men 13.5-17.5 g/dL; Women 12.0-16.0 g/dL. Low Hb indicates anemia.
- **Hematocrit (Hct):** Percentage of blood volume occupied by RBCs. Normal: Men 38-50%; Women 36-44%.
- **White Blood Cell (WBC) Count:** Normal: 4,500-11,000/mcL. High WBC may indicate infection or inflammation.
- **Platelet Count:** Normal: 150,000-400,000/mcL. Low platelets (thrombocytopenia) can cause bleeding. High platelets may indicate inflammation.
- **MCV (Mean Corpuscular Volume):** Size of RBCs. Normal: 80-100 fL. Low MCV suggests iron deficiency; High MCV suggests B12/folate deficiency.
- **MCH & MCHC:** Hemoglobin content of RBCs.
- **RDW (Red Cell Distribution Width):** Variation in RBC size.

### When It Is Ordered
- Routine health checkup
- Suspected anemia, infection, or blood disorders
- Monitoring during chemotherapy or chronic disease
- Pre-surgical evaluation
- Dengue monitoring (platelet count)

## HbA1c (Glycated Hemoglobin)

### What It Is
HbA1c measures average blood sugar levels over the past 2-3 months. It reflects how well diabetes is being managed.

### Normal Ranges
- **Normal:** Below 5.7%
- **Prediabetes:** 5.7% to 6.4%
- **Diabetes:** 6.5% or higher
- **Target for diabetics:** Below 7% (individualized by doctor)

### When It Is Ordered
- Diagnosing diabetes or prediabetes
- Monitoring diabetes management (every 3-6 months)
- Evaluating treatment effectiveness

## Liver Function Test (LFT)

### What It Is
LFT is a group of blood tests that measure enzymes, proteins, and substances produced or processed by the liver.

### Components
- **ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase):** Normal: 7-56 U/L. Elevated in liver damage (hepatitis, fatty liver).
- **AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase):** Normal: 10-40 U/L. Elevated in liver, heart, or muscle damage.
- **ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase):** Normal: 44-147 U/L. Elevated in bile duct obstruction, bone disease.
- **GGT (Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase):** Elevated in alcohol-related liver disease, bile duct problems.
- **Total Bilirubin:** Normal: 0.1-1.2 mg/dL. Elevated in jaundice, liver disease, hemolysis.
- **Direct & Indirect Bilirubin:** Helps differentiate types of jaundice.
- **Albumin:** Normal: 3.5-5.0 g/dL. Low in chronic liver disease, malnutrition.
- **Total Protein:** Normal: 6.0-8.3 g/dL.
- **Prothrombin Time (PT)/INR:** Measures clotting ability. Prolonged in severe liver disease.

### When It Is Ordered
- Suspected liver disease (hepatitis, fatty liver, cirrhosis)
- Monitoring medication effects on liver
- Jaundice evaluation
- Pre-operative assessment
- Chronic alcohol use monitoring

## Kidney Function Test (KFT / Renal Function Test)

### Components
- **Serum Creatinine:** Normal: Men 0.7-1.3 mg/dL; Women 0.6-1.1 mg/dL. Elevated in kidney dysfunction.
- **Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN):** Normal: 7-20 mg/dL. Elevated in kidney disease, dehydration.
- **BUN/Creatinine Ratio:** Helps distinguish kidney from non-kidney causes.
- **Estimated GFR (eGFR):** Normal: above 90 mL/min. Below 60 indicates chronic kidney disease.
- **Uric Acid:** Normal: Men 3.4-7.0 mg/dL; Women 2.4-6.0 mg/dL. Elevated in gout, kidney stones.
- **Electrolytes:** Sodium (135-145 mEq/L), Potassium (3.5-5.0 mEq/L), Chloride, Bicarbonate.

### When It Is Ordered
- Suspected kidney disease
- Diabetes and hypertension monitoring
- Medication monitoring (nephrotoxic drugs)
- Dehydration evaluation
- Pre-operative assessment

## Lipid Profile

### What It Is
A lipid profile measures fats and fatty substances in the blood. Used to assess cardiovascular risk.

### Components
- **Total Cholesterol:** Desirable: below 200 mg/dL; Borderline: 200-239; High: 240+
- **LDL ("Bad") Cholesterol:** Optimal: below 100 mg/dL; Near optimal: 100-129; High: 160+
- **HDL ("Good") Cholesterol:** Desirable: above 60 mg/dL; Low (risk): below 40 mg/dL
- **Triglycerides:** Normal: below 150 mg/dL; Borderline: 150-199; High: 200-499; Very high: 500+
- **VLDL Cholesterol:** Normal: 2-30 mg/dL
- **Total Cholesterol/HDL Ratio:** Below 5 is desirable

### When It Is Ordered
- Cardiovascular risk assessment
- Routine health screening (adults over 20, every 4-6 years)
- Monitoring cholesterol-lowering medication
- Diabetes, hypertension, or obesity evaluation
- Family history of heart disease

## Blood Sugar Tests

### Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS)
- **Normal:** Below 100 mg/dL
- **Prediabetes:** 100-125 mg/dL
- **Diabetes:** 126 mg/dL or higher
- Requires 8-12 hours fasting before the test

### Post-Prandial Blood Sugar (PPBS)
- Taken 2 hours after a meal
- **Normal:** Below 140 mg/dL
- **Diabetes:** 200 mg/dL or higher

### Random Blood Sugar (RBS)
- Can be taken at any time
- **Diabetes:** 200 mg/dL or higher with symptoms

## Thyroid Profile

### Components
- **TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone):** Normal: 0.4-4.0 mIU/L. High TSH: hypothyroidism. Low TSH: hyperthyroidism.
- **Free T4 (Thyroxine):** Normal: 0.8-1.8 ng/dL. Low in hypothyroidism.
- **Free T3 (Triiodothyronine):** Normal: 2.3-4.2 pg/mL.
- **Thyroid Antibodies:** Anti-TPO, Anti-Thyroglobulin for autoimmune thyroid disease.

## ECG (Electrocardiogram)

### What It Is
ECG records the electrical activity of the heart. It is a non-invasive, painless test that takes about 5-10 minutes.

### What It Detects
- Heart rhythm abnormalities (arrhythmias)
- Heart attack (myocardial infarction) — current or previous
- Heart muscle enlargement
- Electrolyte imbalances
- Heart block
- Ischemia (reduced blood flow to heart)

### When It Is Ordered
- Chest pain evaluation
- Shortness of breath
- Palpitations or irregular heartbeat
- Pre-operative assessment
- Monitoring heart conditions
- Routine health checkup for high-risk patients

## Imaging Tests

### X-Ray
- Uses radiation to create images of internal structures
- Common uses: chest X-ray (lungs, heart), bone fractures, joint assessment
- Quick, painless, widely available
- Minimal radiation exposure

### CT Scan (Computed Tomography)
- Uses X-rays from multiple angles to create cross-sectional images
- More detailed than standard X-ray
- Common uses: brain imaging (stroke, tumors), chest/abdomen evaluation, trauma assessment
- May use contrast dye for enhanced images
- Higher radiation dose than X-ray

### MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)
- Uses magnetic fields and radio waves (no radiation)
- Provides detailed images of soft tissues, brain, spinal cord, joints, organs
- Common uses: brain disorders, spinal disc problems, joint injuries, organ evaluation
- Takes 30-90 minutes; requires lying still inside the scanner
- Contraindicated with certain metallic implants (pacemakers, some surgical clips)
- May use gadolinium contrast for enhanced images

### Ultrasound
- Uses sound waves to create real-time images
- No radiation exposure
- Common uses: pregnancy monitoring, abdominal organs (liver, kidneys, gallbladder), thyroid, heart (echocardiogram), pelvic evaluation
- Painless and widely available

## Urine Tests

### Routine Urinalysis
- **Color and Appearance:** Normal: pale to dark yellow, clear
- **pH:** Normal: 4.5-8.0
- **Specific Gravity:** Normal: 1.005-1.030 (concentration)
- **Protein:** Normally absent. Presence may indicate kidney disease.
- **Glucose:** Normally absent. Presence may indicate diabetes.
- **Blood:** Normally absent. Presence may indicate infection, stones, or kidney disease.
- **WBCs/Pus Cells:** Elevated in urinary tract infections (UTI).
- **Bacteria:** Presence indicates UTI.
- **Ketones:** Presence may indicate diabetic ketoacidosis or fasting.
- **Bilirubin:** Presence may indicate liver disease.

### Urine Culture
- Identifies specific bacteria causing UTI
- Determines antibiotic sensitivity
- Ordered when UTI is suspected or recurrent

## Vitamin D Test

### What It Is
Measures 25-hydroxyvitamin D level in the blood.

### Normal Ranges
- **Sufficient:** 30-100 ng/mL
- **Insufficient:** 20-29 ng/mL
- **Deficient:** Below 20 ng/mL
- **Toxic:** Above 100 ng/mL

### Deficiency Symptoms
- Bone pain and weakness
- Muscle weakness
- Fatigue
- Depression
- Frequent infections
- Slow wound healing

### Sources
- Sunlight exposure (15-20 minutes daily)
- Fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, tuna)
- Egg yolks
- Fortified milk and cereals
- Supplements (Vitamin D3 — cholecalciferol)
